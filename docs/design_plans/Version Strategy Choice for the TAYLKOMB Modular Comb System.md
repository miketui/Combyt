# Version Strategy Choice for the TAYLKOMB Modular Comb System

## Executive Overview

The TAYLKOMB system can reasonably be taken in two main "version" directions at the architecture level: a single unified long-length family where most modules live around 220–235 mm, or a dual-family system where one set of modules is shortened for fast cutting/precision work while another remains in the long detangling/sectioning band. The underlying market and ergonomic data favor the dual-family strategy for working stylists, because it preserves the benefits of modularity while restoring the short-comb speed and control that premium cutting combs provide.[^1][^2]

## Relevant Constraints from Existing Audits

The drawing-verified audit shows that current draft assemblies (comb body plus handle minus connector overlap) land in the 405–424 mm overall range, which is 1.7–1.9 times longer than the longest single-piece professional combs on the market. This length inflation comes from stacking a ~208 mm body with ~243 mm handles, even though the connector engagement reduces the numerical sum. The same audit explicitly flags the current handles as oversized and recommends a shorter "compact" handle option to bring assembled lengths down toward a more conventional tool footprint.[^3]

The CAD handoff document then generalizes these findings into target length bands for three families:

- Cutting/precision: 185–205 mm overall
- Tail/sectioning + long styling: 215–235 mm overall
- Detangling/rake/curly: 225–240 mm overall[^2][^1]

This document also introduces an explicit strategic fork for the narrow comb module: either shorten it into the cutting/precision band (Strategy A) or keep it long alongside the wide comb (Strategy B).[^1][^2]

## Strategy A vs Strategy B for Narrow Comb

In Strategy A, the narrow attachment becomes the core "precision/cutting" module with an ideal overall length of about 198 mm and an acceptable band of 185–210 mm. This aligns directly with benchmark cutting combs that typically fall in the 180–191 mm range, restoring the quick, wrist-efficient handling that stylists expect for close-in scissor work.[^2][^1]

In Strategy B, both narrow and wide attachments stay in a single long-length family around 220–230 mm so that all comb modules feel similar in overall reach and balance. While this simplifies muscle memory and keeps the entire modular system visually consistent, it sacrifices the speed advantage of a short cutting comb and reinforces the long-in-hand character already highlighted as a concern in the drawing-verified audit.[^3][^1][^2]

## Ergonomics and Use-Case Alignment

Professional comb usage naturally clusters into different tasks: short, fast tools for cutting and close control, and longer tools for sectioning, detangling, and smoothing longer hair sections. The CAD handoff report encodes this reality by explicitly mapping cutting/precision into the 185–205 mm band and longer styling/sectioning into higher bands, instead of trying to cover all tasks with a single length.[^1][^2]

Keeping everything in the long band would make the TAYLKOMB system feel more like a family of long sectioning/detangling tools, even when a stylist is doing precision cutting work. By contrast, introducing a shorter precision module and companion handle (or compact handle mode) gives stylists a reason to switch configurations when they change tasks, which is the core promise of a modular system.[^3][^1]

## Manufacturing and System-Simplicity Considerations

From a manufacturing standpoint, the connector standard is already shared across modules, so introducing a second length family does not require a second interface standard. Both the drawing audit and the CAD brief emphasize that the 61 × 45.2 × 20.32 mm connector block with consistent rail features is a system-level decision that should remain constant even as lengths are rationalized.[^3][^1]

The main cost of Strategy A is additional SKUs and some extra CAD work, not re-architecting the locking mechanism. Strategy B reduces SKU complexity by keeping everything in the long band but undermines the ergonomic differentiation that the same documents highlight as a key opportunity for TAYLKOMB versus single-piece competitors.[^2][^1][^3]

## Recommended Version Direction

Taken together, the drawing-verified audit and the CAD handoff strongly support a dual-family approach where at least one module (and ideally a matching handle) is shortened into the cutting/precision band while other modules stay in the long detangling/sectioning bands. This "two length families" version delivers clear functional differentiation, recovers short-comb handling for cutting, and still leverages the existing standardized connector architecture to keep engineering complexity manageable.[^1][^2][^3]

Under this recommendation, the narrow comb becomes the primary short precision piece, the wide comb stays in the longer band, and a compact handle variant is introduced to keep assembled lengths in a comfortable range for precision work.[^3][^1]
